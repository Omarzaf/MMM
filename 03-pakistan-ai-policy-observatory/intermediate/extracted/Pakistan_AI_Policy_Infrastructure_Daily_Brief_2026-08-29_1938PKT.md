# 7. What changed / why it matters

What changed: nothing material was verified after the 19:24 PKT cutoff. The live evidence did not advance any item from proposal to approval, approval to funding, tender to award, contract to commissioning, announcement to realised investment, or ceremony to a fully disclosed operational footprint.

Why it matters: the main decision risk today is status inflation. Pakistan has a dense pipeline of AI, sovereign-cloud, data-exchange, skills and infrastructure announcements, but relatively few public artefacts showing financing, technical capacity, binding safeguards, delivery, acceptance or performance. Readers should price implementation probability from operative documents and measurable milestones, not announcement volume.

- **Companies and cloud/AI vendors:** Use project-level privacy, security, localization/transfer, subprocessor, audit, resilience and exit terms. Do not treat draft policy as settled law or “sovereign” as a substitute for disclosed processing locations and control rights.

- **Public bodies:** Publish lawful authority, purpose, data inventories, role allocation, risk/impact assessments, human review, procurement criteria, incident processes, acceptance tests, complaints and redress for production AI and shared-data systems.

- **Investors and lenders:** Demand signed instruments, financing sources, site rights, energy and network design, equipment/capacity lists, implementation calendars, customer/workload evidence, certification and acceptance before treating announced facilities as operating assets.

- **Users and civil society:** Track whether data-use, correction, appeal, deletion, breach notice and automated-decision safeguards are actually available at service level while the horizontal privacy framework remains draft.

## Confidence note

Medium confidence applies to broad absence findings and implementation gaps because the comparison window is narrow, some pages are mutable or date-only, PTA timed out in one track, and publication/indexing can lag. Low confidence is used only where implementation is inferred from promotional or forward-looking language without operative documents.
