# 2. Policy, regulation, and governance

**National AI Policy remains approved; no new implementation instrument located.**

**Status:** Approved policy; implementation mechanisms not newly verified.

**Institution / location:** Ministry of IT & Telecommunication (MoITT), federal / Islamabad.

**Evidence:** MoITT’s live policy register continues to list the National Artificial Intelligence Policy as Approved, approval date 31 July 2025.

**What changed:** None since the previous cutoff. No new AI Council/directorate instrument, binding standard, regulatory sandbox rule, model register, impact-assessment procedure, audit framework or redress mechanism was found.

**Why it matters:** Organizations should treat the policy as strategic direction, not as proof that every governance mechanism or funding vehicle described in it is operational.

**Confidence: High for register status; medium for the negative implementation finding because publication can lag.**

**National Data Governance Policy 2026 remains draft/finalisation-stage.**

**Status:** Draft; consultation closed; final approval/publication not verified.

**Institution / location:** MoITT and Pakistan Digital Authority (PDA), federal / Islamabad.

**Evidence:** MoITT still labels the policy Draft with a 26 June 2026 date. PDA reported on 5 August that consultations had concluded and the policy had moved to its final stage.

**What changed:** None after 19:52 PKT. No revised final text, competent-body approval, formal publication, commencement date, implementation schedule or additional PDA rule surfaced.

**Why it matters:** Public bodies should prepare for stronger data inventory, sharing, privacy, security and automated-decision governance, but companies and agencies should not cite the draft as an enacted general personal-data law.

**Confidence: High for current labels; medium for absence of an unpublished approval.**

# 3. Data regulation, digital policy, and cybersecurity

**Virtual-asset data and cyber obligations remain the clearest new sector-specific compliance baseline.**

**Status:** Regulations notified 21 August 2026; portal open; no post-cutoff licence or enforcement update.

**Institution / location:** Pakistan Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (PVARA), federal.

**Evidence:** PVARA continues to publish S.R.O. 1419(I)/2026 and S.R.O. 1420(I)/2026 and states that applications are open for sandbox entry, NOCs and VASP licences. Its live guidance requires local incorporation, AML/CFT controls, cybersecurity, business continuity and at least ten years of records; transitional operators face a 5 September NOC deadline.

**What changed:** No additional regulation, NOC, licence, sandbox admission or enforcement action was verified in the interval.

**Why it matters:** Virtual-asset businesses have concrete near-term compliance work even though Pakistan’s generally applicable personal-data regime remains unresolved. Cloud outsourcing, personal/financial data controls, incident handling, auditability and local-entity accountability warrant board-level attention.

**Confidence: High for published instruments and portal requirements; medium for market-wide implementation.**

**No new national cyber advisory or general digital-policy decision.**

**Status:** No change verified.

**Institution / location:** National CERT (PKCERT), MoITT, PDA and PTA.

**Evidence:** PKCERT’s 2026 list remains capped at Advisory 15. MoITT’s policy register and PDA newsroom showed no post-cutoff addition; the federal PID feed remained capped at PR 266 at 12:54:47 PKT, before the prior cutoff.

**What changed:** None. The absence of a new advisory is not evidence that threat conditions are static; it only means no new official publication was located.

**Why it matters:** Operators should continue existing patching, monitoring and incident-response duties and avoid inferring regulatory relief from a quiet publication window.

**Confidence: High for the visible official lists; medium-high overall due to indexing lag.**

# 9. Method and limitations

The monitor compared live official registers and publication feeds against the previous briefing’s approximately 19:52 PKT cutoff, then ran targeted searches across policy, data, cyber, procurement, infrastructure, provincial and Google-presence tracks. Search snippets were not treated as proof; consequential status claims were checked against opened official pages wherever possible.

The interval is exceptionally narrow. PPRA and some provincial systems expose date-only or stale metadata, while publication and indexing can lag the underlying event. Confidence therefore measures evidence quality and timestamp precision, not the importance of the topic.
