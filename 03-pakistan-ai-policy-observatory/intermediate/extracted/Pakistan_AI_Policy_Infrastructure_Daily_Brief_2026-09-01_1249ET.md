# 7. What changed / why it matters

- What changed: nothing material was verified in the approximately 11-minute interval. The report therefore does not promote late-indexed speeches, event pages, date-only articles, tenders, or repeated announcements into implementation milestones.

- Legal effect: Pakistan still lacks a verified horizontal personal-data statute in force. The AI Policy is approved policy, the Data Governance Policy remains draft, the child social-media measures remain political/procedural, and the PISF’s precise implementation mechanics remain incompletely documented.

- Commercial effect: planned public compute and sovereign-cloud initiatives remain before final approval, contract, capacity allocation, or customer evidence. Investors should discount headline project values until ECNEC decisions, releases, awards, hardware, power and operating metrics are visible.

- Operational effect: agencies proceeding with AI, identity, education-record, cloud, and data-exchange projects need interim governance in contracts and system design: lawful authority, purpose limits, data minimisation, role allocation, security controls, human review, retention/deletion, incident response, audit, redress, portability, and exit.

- Evidence risk: confidence is strongest for what the live primary registers display and weaker for proving a universal absence during a short window. Mutable pages, unavailable PTA pages, date-only releases, and indexing lag can produce later corrections.
