# 8. What changed / why it matters

The strict answer is that nothing material changed in the 32 minutes since the previous briefing. The decision-relevant finding is therefore status discipline: policy remains policy, drafts remain drafts, tenders remain procurement-stage records, CDWP recommendations remain short of ECNEC approval, and ministerial investment pitches remain short of facility-readiness proof.

- For companies: do not assume a comprehensive horizontal privacy act is in force. Continue to map sector rules, contractual data obligations, cybersecurity controls and expected future transfer/localisation requirements.

- For public bodies: WASL and DNP standards are a concrete interoperability and consent-design baseline, but adoption, conformance and legal incorporation should be verified instrument by instrument.

- For investors and cloud/data-centre operators: require facility-level power, cooling, connectivity, land, permitting, financing and commissioning evidence before valuing announced sovereign compute capacity.

- For suppliers: the watched tenders are opportunities, not revenue. Evaluation, award, contract publication, acceptance and payment remain separate gates.

- For users and civil society: the absence of a horizontal privacy law leaves important questions unresolved, including lawful bases, rights, children’s data, automated decisions, cross-border transfers, government access, breach notification and remedies.
