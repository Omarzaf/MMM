# 3. Data regulation, digital policy, and cybersecurity

## Data Governance Policy 2026 remains draft/finalisation-stage

**Development.** No notification, final text or commencement instrument appeared. **Status.** Draft; consultation concluded and feedback incorporation stated. **Institution/location.** Pakistan Digital Authority and MoITT, Islamabad.

**Evidence.** PDA’s 5 August release says the policy moved to the final stage but also says PDA will incorporate stakeholder feedback before finalising it. The MoITT register still labels it Draft. [PDA consultation conclusion (5 August 2026)](https://pda.gov.pk/press-details.php?id=pda-dgp-consultation); [MoITT policy register](https://www.moitt.gov.pk/Policies).

**What changed.** Nothing after the cutoff. **Why it matters.** The draft does not itself establish enforceable government-wide sharing, localisation, retention, breach-notification or cross-border-transfer duties. Public bodies should not treat planned WASL interoperability as authority to share complete datasets. Confidence: high.

## No enacted horizontal personal-data law; pilot standards unchanged

**Development.** No authenticated successor personal-data bill or enacted horizontal law surfaced. **Status.** Legislative gap persists; older drafts are not law. DNP publications remain Pilot.

**Evidence.** MoITT’s legislation register still exposes older personal-data drafts; the Senate private-bills register records the 2023 bill as disposed/withdrawn. The DNP register still lists four publications, all at Pilot maturity, dated 26 April or 13 May 2026. [MoITT legislation register](https://www.moitt.gov.pk/Legislations); [Senate private-bills register](https://senate.gov.pk/en/pbs.php?catid=1&cattitle=Bills&leftcatid=278&subcatid=276); [DNP publications register](https://standards.dnp.gov.pk/register.html).

> **Practical obligations now.** No new generally applicable duty was created in this window. Companies and public bodies must still map applicable sectoral, constitutional, contractual and cybersecurity requirements. For AI/data procurements, contracts should specify controller/processor roles, legal purpose and authority, hosting and model provider, subprocessors and transfers, retention/deletion, access controls, incident response, audit, automated-scoring review, human appeal and redress. Investors should diligence these controls rather than treating “sovereign” or “secure” marketing language as compliance evidence.

## Cybersecurity register unchanged

# 7. What changed / why it matters

- **The only observed numerical delta was PPRA +1 active/recent tender.** Because the portal is mutable and date-only, the movement is a discovery signal, not evidence that a monitored project advanced. Evaluation and contract totals were flat.

- **Legal and governance exposure did not change.** There is still no enacted horizontal personal-data statute or notified Data Governance Policy. Organisations should base current compliance on applicable existing law, sector rules and contracts, and should not infer authority from policy announcements.

- **Implementation risk remains concentrated in missing instruments.** The National AI Policy’s ambitions need named owners, budgets, rules, standards, capacity and deadlines. Without those, implementation likelihood cannot be judged from announcements alone.

- **Infrastructure claims remain difficult to compare.** Procurement-stage and company-reported milestones lack common evidence for accepted compute, utilisation, uptime, residency, cybersecurity, grid connection, cooling, water and realised capital expenditure.

- **The narrow window limits absence claims.** A 15-minute comparison is highly sensitive to publication latency and pages without timestamps. High confidence attaches to inspected register states; confidence is lower on the proposition that no unpublished or unindexed event occurred.
