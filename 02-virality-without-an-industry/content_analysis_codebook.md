# Content Analysis Codebook

**Corpus:** 120 tracks and associated official music videos, selected from the 360-track research sample  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 23 August 2026

## 1. Analytical purpose

The content analysis tests how musical and visual popular culture changed alongside Pakistan's platform economy. It does not infer an artist's beliefs, a listener's interpretation, or a social group's behaviour from a song alone. The unit of analysis is representation within a recorded work and its official visual presentation.

## 2. Sampling

Select 10 tracks from each complete year, 2014-2025. Within each year, preserve variation across the six primary sampling strata, language, genre, gender configuration, region, and institutional support. A track can be culturally important without being among the year's most viewed; popularity and analytical significance are different variables.

For each selected work, code:

1. The commercially or publicly released audio version.
2. The official music video, live-session video, visualizer, or branded performance designated in the track dataset.
3. Public credits and release metadata.
4. Lyrics only from licensed, artist-supplied, or research-transcribed material. Do not reproduce full lyrics in research outputs.

## 3. Coding workflow

1. Two coders independently code a 20-item pilot balanced across years and languages.
2. Reconcile definitions, not individual opinions, after the pilot.
3. Double-code at least 25 percent of the final corpus, stratified across years and languages.
4. Target agreement of at least 0.80 for variables used in confirmatory comparisons. Variables between 0.67 and 0.79 remain exploratory and require a disagreement note. Revise or exclude variables below 0.67.
5. Coders record uncertainty and evidence snippets in private notes rather than forcing ambiguous content into a confident category.

## 4. General coding scale

Unless a variable specifies otherwise, use:

- **0 Absent:** no meaningful instance.
- **1 Present but minor:** appears briefly or peripherally.
- **2 Prominent:** recurring or materially important.
- **3 Central:** organizes the track's narrative, address, or visual concept.
- **8 Not applicable:** the code cannot logically apply to the available item.
- **9 Unknown/unclear:** evidence is unavailable, untranslated, contradictory, or too ambiguous.

Do not collapse 8 and 9. Not applicable and unknown are analytically different, despite spreadsheets' determined campaign to make them indistinguishable.

## 5. Audio, language, and genre variables

| Code | Definition | Values / notes |
|---|---|---|
| `primary_language` | Language carrying the largest share of meaningful lyrics | Use standardized language list; instrumental is allowed |
| `secondary_languages` | Other languages used meaningfully | Multi-select; do not count isolated brand names |
| `language_switching_function` | What switching appears to do | none; rhyme; prestige/cosmopolitanism; intimacy; regional marking; humour; quotation; unclear |
| `primary_genre` | Dominant genre identity in release metadata and expert coding | Use project taxonomy; record disagreement |
| `genre_hybridity` | Degree to which multiple genre traditions organize the work | 0-3 |
| `folk_or_classical_reference` | Audible reference to identifiable folk, devotional, classical, or regional forms | 0-3; name form only with evidence |
| `electronic_production_salience` | Prominence of digitally programmed or synthesized production | 0-3 |
| `platform_optimized_structure` | Observable structural features often associated with platform circulation | short intro; early hook; short duration; loopable ending; serialized remix; none; cannot infer intent |
| `collaboration_structure` | Configuration of credited performers | solo; feature; duet; ensemble; rotating platform cast |

A structural feature is not proof that an artist intentionally optimized for an algorithm. Intent belongs in interviews.

## 6. Lyrical themes

Code each 0-3 and add a brief evidence note.

- `romantic_intimacy`: attraction, attachment, union, longing.
- `heartbreak_loss`: separation, betrayal, mourning, emotional absence.
- `desire_sensuality`: bodily desire, seduction, erotic suggestion.
- `self_assertion`: confidence, recognition, status, boundary setting.
- `economic_aspiration`: wealth, mobility, work, success, luxury, or escape from scarcity.
- `economic_precarity`: unemployment, debt, insecurity, exploitation, price pressure, survival.
- `urban_life`: city movement, neighbourhood identity, congestion, nightlife, street culture.
- `rural_or_pastoral_life`: village, land, agricultural life, pastoral memory.
- `migration_diaspora`: departure, distance, home, border, foreign life, return.
- `class_identity`: explicit or implied class position, consumption, respectability, elite exclusion.
- `gender_role`: prescriptive or contested femininity, masculinity, marriage, family authority.
- `female_autonomy`: women's agency, refusal, desire, work, mobility, or self-definition.
- `masculine_vulnerability`: grief, fear, tenderness, insecurity, or emotional dependence voiced through masculinity.
- `religious_devotion`: prayer, praise, divine love, sacred figures, ritual, or theological imagery.
- `mystical_sufi_reference`: Sufi poetry, saints, metaphors, shrine culture, or ecstatic devotion.
- `national_identity`: Pakistan, nation, flag, military, collective destiny, patriotic belonging.
- `regional_ethnic_identity`: named or symbolically explicit Baloch, Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashtun, Saraiki, Kashmiri, Gilgit-Baltistan, or other identity.
- `cross_border_affinity`: kinship, shared culture, peace, or relational address across national borders.
- `political_critique`: state, elite, violence, censorship, injustice, or institutional criticism.
- `protest_resistance`: mobilization, refusal, rebellion, solidarity, liberation.
- `mental_health_introspection`: anxiety, depression, isolation, self-doubt, healing, interiority.
- `nostalgia_archive`: past eras, lost places, inherited culture, memory, revival.
- `technology_platform_life`: phones, social media, online fame, surveillance, digital relationships.
- `humour_satire`: comic framing, parody, irony, social satire.

## 8. Visual representation

Code the official visual item only. If no official video exists, mark video variables not applicable rather than substituting an unofficial fan upload.

### Setting and geography

- `urban_metropolitan_setting`: 0-3.
- `working_class_neighbourhood_setting`: 0-3.
- `rural_landscape_setting`: 0-3.
- `heritage_architecture`: 0-3.
- `studio_or_stage_setting`: 0-3.
- `transnational_location`: 0-3.
- `place_specificity`: generic; named city/region; visually recognizable but unnamed; unclear.

### Class and consumption

- `luxury_consumption`: cars, brands, high-end interiors, travel, jewellery, exclusive leisure.
- `ordinary_domestic_life`: homes, streets, work, transport, and non-elite routine.
- `labour_visibility`: bodies engaged in paid or unpaid work.
- `class_contrast`: explicit juxtaposition of wealth, poverty, status, or access.
- `aspirational_aesthetic`: visual organization around mobility, polish, glamour, or elite belonging.

### Gender, body, and social relations

- `women_screen_centrality`: background; supporting; co-equal; central; absent.
- `women_narrative_agency`: 0-3.
- `male_dominance_display`: 0-3.
- `gender_nonconformity_or_queer_coding`: 0-3; code representation only, never infer a performer's identity.
- `sexualization`: 0-3, distinguishing self-directed performance from objectifying camera treatment in notes.
- `family_respectability_frame`: 0-3.
- `harassment_or_violence`: absent; implied; shown; central; unclear.

### Region, tradition, and authenticity

- `regional_clothing_or_material_culture`: 0-3.
- `folk_instrument_visibility`: 0-3.
- `ritual_or_devotional_space`: 0-3.
- `regional_people_as_background`: 0-3.
- `artist_control_of_regional_representation`: clear; shared; externally curated; unknown.
- `touristic_or_exoticizing_gaze`: 0-3 with written justification.

### Corporate and platform visibility

- `brand_presence`: none; end-card only; integrated product; programme identity; central narrative object.
- `platform_interface_visibility`: 0-3.
- `sponsor_credit_prominence`: none; low; moderate; high.
- `artist_credit_prominence`: none; low; moderate; high.
- `producer_credit_prominence`: none; low; moderate; high.

## 10. Audience-comment module, optional

Audience comments are a separate unit and require separate sampling. Do not treat the top comments under one viral video as public opinion.

For each selected video, draw a reproducible comment sample by date and sort mode where platform access permits. Record deleted/disabled comments, language, apparent bot or promotional repetition, cross-border identity claims, praise, criticism, political conflict, gendered abuse, and interpretation of regional or religious symbols. Never attempt to deanonymize users.

## 11. Translation protocol

1. Preserve the original-language coding note.
2. Store a literal translation and an interpretive gloss separately.
3. Have culturally specific metaphors reviewed by a speaker familiar with the relevant region and genre.
4. Record uncertain phrases; do not resolve ambiguity through automated translation alone.
5. Do not publish full translated lyrics without permission. Quote only the minimum necessary phrase under applicable research and copyright rules.

## 12. Reliability and adjudication

The adjudication memo must identify:

- Variables with persistent disagreement.
- Languages or genres producing systematic uncertainty.
- Whether disagreements arise from definition, translation, missing visuals, or genuine polysemy.
- Any variable removed or downgraded to exploratory status.
- Examples used as anchors for each ordinal scale.

The final report must publish reliability statistics and missingness, not merely announce that coders were "trained." That phrase has covered an impressive range of methodological sins.
